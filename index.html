The short version

The store has a strong base: a real ranch story, good product photos, local pickup at the ranch in Elon, and fast payment options like Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and Venmo.

The biggest issue is stock. Only 2 of 26 beef cuts can be bought right now (Ribeye and Tenderloin), and 3 of the 4 "Best Sellers" on the homepage show "Sold out."

The second issue is trust. The top banner says "We Ship Nationwide," but the shipping and refund policy pages do not exist (they show "page not found"), and nothing on the site explains frozen shipping, packaging, minimums or delivery areas.

Most fixes are small and can be done in the Shopify admin in a few hours. The bigger gains come from price per pound, bundles, back-in-stock alerts and email flows.

Fix now

1. Most beef is sold out, and the homepage leads with sold-out items

What I saw: Only American Wagyu Ribeye Steak ($45) and Tenderloin Steak ($48) can be bought. Breakfast Sausage Links, New York Strip and Chuck Roast all show "Sold out" in the homepage Best Sellers. Sold-out pages offer no way to get notified.

Why it matters: New visitors hit dead ends and leave, and each sold-out page is a missed chance to collect an email.

Fix: Show in-stock items first, add "Notify me when back" (Klaviyo is already installed), and say when the next batch is coming.

Effort: Low1 to 3 hours

2. No shipping policy and no refund policy

What I saw: Both policy pages return "page not found." The Terms of Service still says "our Refund Policy [LINK]" (an unfilled template spot). No page explains frozen shipping, dry ice, ship days, transit time, minimums, cost or delivery area.

Why it matters: Frozen meat is a trust purchase. Google Shopping and Meta Shops also expect these policies before approving listings.

Fix: Add both policies in Shopify settings, fix the [LINK], and add a short "How shipping works" block on product pages (see mockup below).

Effort: LowRanch supplies the real rules

3. Stray code inside skincare descriptions

What I saw: Four skincare products (Face Balm, Gold Edition Face Balm, Honey Moisturizer, Cool Mint Lip Balm) and the Skincare collection each have about 12 KB of pasted page code at the top of the description, including rules that can change the whole page layout. On the Face Balm page one sentence is split mid-word.

Why it matters: It can affect how pages look, adds weight, and Google Shopping or Meta catalogs may pick up code instead of a description.

Fix: Delete the code block in the HTML view and re-paste the text as plain text.

Effort: LowUnder 1 hour

4. Price and option mistakes

  • Porterhouse: the larger 20 to 24 oz option ($45) costs less than 14 to 18 oz ($59).
  • 5oz Patties: has a "7 oz" option at the same $9.75, but the title says 5 oz.
  • Hangar Steak: the "compare at" price ($24) is lower than the price ($29).
  • Stir Fry and Kabobs: the same $22 price shows twice.
  • Ribeye: description says both "Bone In" and "Boneless approximately 12oz"; the size option says 12oz to 16oz.
Effort: Low30 minutes

5. Two different contact emails

The Terms list info@circlehwagyu.com and the Privacy Policy lists a Gmail address. Product data also uses two brand names ("Circle H Ranch" and "Circle H Wagyu"). Pick one of each.

Effort: Low30 minutes

Quick wins

6. Show price per pound and pack details

The price-per-unit slot is empty on every product, and product weights in Shopify are inconsistent (some are 0, many are set to exactly 1 lb). Using the store's own numbers: Ribeye at $45 for 12 to 16 oz is about $45 to $60 per lb; Tenderloin at $48 for 8 to 12 oz is about $64 to $96 per lb. Buyers compare these numbers across ranches.

Effort: Low to Medium2 to 4 hours

7. Search basics: headings and titles

The homepage has an empty main heading (H1) and its page title is only "Circle H Wagyu." The Our Ranch page has no main heading. Collection headings read "Collection: American Wagyu Beef." Adding "Alamance County, NC" and "American Wagyu" to these helps local searches.

Effort: Low1 hour

8. Add image descriptions (alt text)

None of the product photos have alt text, and 6 of 11 homepage images are blank. This helps Google Images and shoppers using screen readers.

Effort: Low1 to 2 hours

9. Refresh dated content

  • Our Ranch page: "Farm tours will be available spring of 2025!"
  • Gift Certificate: "stocking stuffers ... this year," with a 2023 screenshot as the photo.
  • The "News" blog has no posts.
  • The email signup promises "bundles," and a review mentions a holiday bundle, but none are for sale.
Effort: Low1 hour

10. Typos

"Hangar Steak" (the cut is Hanger, which the description uses), "al natural," "knowledgable," "we dedicate ourselves on raising," "personal around the clock care," "11oz's."

Effort: Low20 minutes

11. Turn on "Buy it now" buttons

Product pages show Add to cart and Shop Pay installments, but no one-tap Shop Pay or Apple Pay button. The cart has them. One setting in the theme.

Effort: Low10 minutes

12. Turn on an email signup form

Klaviyo is installed and loads on every page, but no Klaviyo signup form is active. The only signup is a small box in the footer. No SMS signup.

Effort: Low to Medium2 to 3 hours

Bigger bets

13. Faster mobile pages

Homepage: main image appeared at 10.2 seconds, page size 2,119 KiB. Ribeye page: 7.5 seconds, with 910 ms of blocking scripts, page size 2,339 KiB. The homepage hero is set to load late (lazy), and the Meta (Facebook) pixel was the largest outside script at about 207 KB. The theme is Dawn 11.0.0 from 2023; newer versions exist.

Effort: MediumHalf day, more for a theme update

14. Bundles, boxes and subscriptions

No bundles or boxes are for sale. A grill box or freezer box raises order size and makes frozen shipping pay for itself. Subscriptions bring repeat orders.

Effort: Medium

15. Real reviews and trust details

The homepage has 3 hand-typed testimonials. No reviews app, no star ratings on products, no marbling or grading info, no processing details, no guarantee. A reviews app with automatic review requests fixes most of this.

Effort: Medium

16. Analytics and Google Shopping

The Meta pixel is active. No Google Analytics 4 or Google Ads tag was found in the site code. Adding Shopify's Google and YouTube app gives GA4 plus free Google Shopping listings (needs the policies from item 2).

Effort: Low to Medium

17. Restaurant and bulk orders

There is a Buy Livestock page for cattle, but nothing for restaurants, bulk orders or quarter and half beef. A simple inquiry page could open a new sales channel.

Effort: Medium

Couldn't check from outside: checkout steps, real shipping rates and zones, which Klaviyo emails are running, Search Console data, and whether the gift certificate is a true Shopify gift card. Apps seen in the page code: Klaviyo, Shopify Forms, Facebook and Instagram (Meta pixel), Shop Pay, Shopify local pickup. All 47 internal links I tested worked.

How I can help

I'm Austin with Pilot Automations. I already know this store, so I can take these on in small, fixed-scope pieces. Here's what I'd suggest:

  • Store cleanup sprint. I'll fix the skincare descriptions, prices, options and typos, put in-stock items first, and draft shipping and refund policies for you to approve.
  • Product page rewrite. A consistent layout for every cut with weight, price per lb, pack size, packaging and cooking tips, plus correct weights in Shopify so shipping rates are right.
  • Search fixes. Page titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text, local search for Alamance County and the Triad, and Google Search Console and Merchant Center setup.
  • Email and text flows. In your Klaviyo account: signup form, welcome emails, abandoned cart reminders, back-in-stock alerts, review requests and new harvest announcements.
  • Bundles and boxes. Grill boxes, freezer boxes and gift bundles, and a subscription option if you want repeat orders.
  • Speed and app cleanup. Faster homepage image, smaller images, a check on the Meta pixel, and a plan for a theme update.
  • Analytics. Google Analytics 4, a check that Meta purchase tracking works, and a simple monthly sales and traffic report.
  • Automations. Low-stock alerts to you, automatic notices when items sell out, "your pickup is ready" messages, and a restaurant and bulk inquiry form that goes straight to your inbox.
